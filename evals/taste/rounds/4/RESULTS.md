(`skill-r3`): `literal`, the first build from the brief as written, and `read`, rebuilt from
a product read argued over three rounds with simulated customers
(`../../intuition/product-read/`). Nine blind pairs and one repeat, graded on two
questions: which would you rather use for this job, and which is more refined.

## Where the read won: scope

tallyho's week, cut from a dozen panels of money and sums to the timer, the day and the
week's hours: "much cleaner to process quickly… there's my calendar… I can see what I've
worked on this week"; at 80×24, "exactly what I want. I'm on Thursday. These are the
things I'm working on." burrow's one-line list at 80×24 won on both questions.

## Where it lost, and why

None of the four losses was the product read's scope. Each traces to one of four causes:

- **A brand motif made into a row marker** (n01, n02). nestkeeper's egg dots, the logo's
  motif, also marked every staged row: "clever, it's pretty. And then we start using it
  all throughout everywhere… a challenge navigating the view." The grader preferred the
  read's settings otherwise: "only need to fix the blue circle as the indicator."
- **An unclear value cut instead of labelled** (b01, b02). The first build's "3 min" read
  as either received time or reading time. The read deleted it; the grader wanted it kept
  and named: "missing the read time, but maybe as a header for the email?"
- **Answering each objection on its own put one thing on screen twice** (b03). The reply
  shows the original beside the draft and again as the editable quote inside it.
- **The mark where it means nothing** (b01): the burrow door beside a letter's header
  "doesn't make sense"; the first build's footer mark: "pretty, love it".

## Asked for again

tallyho's agents: "how am I tracking agents doing work?… that's actual cost." The read cut
them because the brief didn't ask. The grader raised it before the read and again after.
Also: "space to stop" needs a pause symbol on the timer line.
